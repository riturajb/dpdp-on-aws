# Privacy office and Data Protection Officer: mandate

A template, filled in for Fernleaf Finance (fictional). It has two parts. Part A applies to every company. Part B applies only if the company is notified as a Significant Data Fiduciary.

---

## What the law requires, and what it does not

| Company | What the law requires | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Every Data Fiduciary | Publish the business contact information of a person who can answer questions about the processing of personal data, prominently on the website or app, and in every reply to a request to exercise rights | Act s.8(9); Rule 9 |
| Every Data Fiduciary | An effective mechanism to redress grievances, with a published reply period of no more than 90 days | Act s.8(10), s.13; Rule 14(3) |
| Significant Data Fiduciary only | Appoint a Data Protection Officer who represents the company under the Act, is based in India, is an individual responsible to the board of directors or similar governing body, and is the point of contact for the grievance redressal mechanism | Act s.10(2)(a) |
| Significant Data Fiduciary only | Appoint an independent data auditor; carry out a Data Protection Impact Assessment and an audit every twelve months, and send the significant observations to the Data Protection Board | Act s.10(2)(b), s.10(2)(c); Rule 13(1), 13(2) |

**A company that is not a Significant Data Fiduciary does not need a Data Protection Officer.** It needs a person who can answer questions about its processing, whose contact details it publishes. This template calls that person the **Privacy Lead**. A company may still choose to call the role Data Protection Officer; if it does, it should make sure the title does not suggest a statutory appointment it has not made.

## Part A. The Privacy Lead (every company)

### 1. Purpose of the role

Runs the company's compliance with the Act and the Rules day to day, under the accountability of the CEO set out in the governance charter, and is the published contact for questions about processing (Section 8(9), Rule 9).

### 2. Responsibilities

- Keep the personal data register: every category of personal data, where it is stored, its purpose, its legal basis, who receives it and how long it is kept.
- Own notices, consent and its withdrawal, rights requests, grievances, retention and erasure, and the rules for children's data (see `raci.xlsx` for each row of the Obligations Matrix).
- Prepare the intimations of a personal data breach for the CEO's approval, and send them (Rule 7).
- Chair the privacy committee; prepare the quarterly report to the board of directors.
- Advise on every decision listed in the governance charter, and agree to those where the charter says "Privacy Lead must agree".
- Answer questions from Data Principals about processing; their contact details are published under Rule 9.
- Keep the policy index current (`policy-index.md`).

### 3. Authority

- Access to every system, record and person needed to carry out the role.
- May require that a new processing activity does not start until its purpose and legal basis are recorded in the register.
- May escalate directly to the CEO, and to the Chair of the Audit Committee without going through the CEO, when the Privacy Lead believes a matter is not being dealt with.
- Approves breach intimations when the CEO cannot be reached within two hours (governance charter, section 5).

### 4. Reporting line and independence

- Reports to the CEO, with direct access to the Audit Committee.
- Should not also be the owner of a business line whose processing the role oversees, or the CISO: the role reviews decisions those roles take.
- Performance objectives are set by the CEO and reviewed by the Chair of the Audit Committee.

### 5. Resources

- A budget for tools, legal advice and training, approved every year.
- Named contacts in each business line, IT, security, legal, HR and procurement.

### 6. Published contact details (Rule 9)

| Item | Fernleaf Finance |
|---|---|
| Who | Head of Privacy (the Privacy Lead) |
| Contact | A role-based email address and a postal address, not a personal mailbox, so that the contact survives a change of person |
| Where published | The privacy page of the website and the app, linked from the footer and the app menu |
| In replies | Included in every template used to reply to a request to exercise rights |

## Part B. The Data Protection Officer (Significant Data Fiduciary only)

If notified, the Privacy Lead's role becomes that of the Data Protection Officer, with these additions required by Section 10(2)(a):

| Requirement | How Fernleaf Finance would meet it |
|---|---|
| Represents the company under the Act | Board resolution appointing the Data Protection Officer and stating this |
| Based in India | The role holder is employed in Mumbai |
| An individual responsible to the board of directors | Responsible to the board of directors for this role, and presents to it directly; reports to the CEO for administration only |
| Point of contact for the grievance redressal mechanism | Named as the escalation point in the published grievance procedure |

And, separately from the Data Protection Officer:

- **Independent data auditor** (Section 10(2)(b)): appointed by the board of directors; independent of the Data Protection Officer and of Internal Audit's day-to-day work.
- **Data Protection Impact Assessment and audit every twelve months** from the date of notification, with a report of significant observations to the Data Protection Board (Rule 13(1), 13(2)). See `dpia-template.md`.
- **Due diligence on algorithmic software** used to process personal data (Rule 13(3)), and any restriction on transferring data specified by the Government (Rule 13(4)).
